Diagnosing Attack Taxonomy Utility in Rare
IoT Intrusion Detection
ABSTRACT
Attack taxonomies may improve rare class detection, but gains can reflect added capacity
rather than useful grouping. We evaluate a diagnostic protocol using grouped validation, equally
privileged wrong mappings, calibrated flat ensembles, a converged stacker, and external tests.
Across six CICIoT2023 public subsample holdouts, equal taxonomy blending improved Macro
F1 by 0.0218 and rare recall by 0.0827. Approximate intervals allowing for repeated holdout
overlap were 0.0208 to 0.0229 and 0.0769 to 0.0885. All six effects were positive, although
multiplicity adjusted sign references were inconclusive. Feature induced partitions recovered
some gain; generic descriptions did not. IoTID20 and archive controls did not reproduce
assignment superiority. Grouped NetFlow allocations favoured stacking on Macro F1;
chronological label shift prevented rare class assessment. The contribution is an auditable local
assessment protocol, not a universally superior classifier or proof of semantic causation.
Keywords: Internet of Things; intrusion detection; attack taxonomy; class imbalance; rare
class recall; grouped validation; probability calibration; diagnostic protocol.
INTRODUCTION
Internet of Things (IoT) deployments combine heterogeneous sensors, gateways, and services,
creating an uneven attack surface (Al-Fuqaha et al., 2015). Fine attack labels can require
different responses, yet high accuracy can conceal rare class misses. Macro F1, class recall, and
precision recall analysis complement accuracy under imbalance (Davis & Goadrich, 2006; He &
Garcia, 2009).
Weighting, resampling, and focal objectives address class imbalance (Chawla et al., 2002;
Han et al., 2005; Mulyanto et al., 2020). Hierarchies add label structure but also capacity and
error propagation (Han et al., 2026; Silla & Freitas, 2011; Uddin et al., 2025). Duplicates, label
conflicts, and capture artefacts further complicate attribution (Arp et al., 2024; Kapoor &
Narayanan, 2023; Kaufman et al., 2012).
We assess whether a supplied taxonomy adds predictive value beyond learner capacity and
decomposition, using matched wrong maps, flat ensembles, feature partitions and mapping
similarity diagnostics.
Discovery uses 1,429,753 CICIoT2023 public subsample rows, 34 labels and 46 numeric
predictors, not the full corpus. IoTID20, official archive files and an independent timestamped
corpus test transfer; N-BaIoT assesses device difficulty, not taxonomy utility.
RQ1 tests Macro F1 and rare recall against weighted flat, ordinary and calibrated blends and a
converged stacker. RQ2 tests assignment specificity against equally privileged wrong maps,
feature and random partitions, generic descriptions and mapping similarity. RQ3 examines
predictor projection, external sources, chronology, conflicts and costs.
The contribution is a controlled local assessment protocol with competitive controls and
explicit adoption boundaries; label consistency and feature alignment remain candidate
moderators, not established sufficient conditions.
RELATED WORK
IoT intrusion detection benchmarks and data provenance
CICIoT2023 (Neto et al., 2023), IoTID20 (Ullah & Mahmoud, 2020), N-BaIoT (Meidan et
al., 2018), and timestamped NF-ToN-IoT-v3 (Luay, 2025; Luay et al., 2026) differ in capture
and validation units. Their aggregate scores are not interchangeable; predictive contrasts require
common assessment records, predictors, roles, and metrics.
Provenance affects apparent difficulty and model ranking: leakage and label errors are
documented across intrusion benchmarks (Arp et al., 2024; Bouke & Abdullah, 2023; Cantone et
al., 2024; Engelen et al., 2021; Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023; Kaufman et al., 2012; Liu et al.,
2022; Zhao et al., 2026). We audit representation, conflicts, and role assignment before
interpreting a map effect.
Learning from class imbalance
Imbalance handling changes sample support or training objectives, including in offline sensor
monitoring (Fu & Han, 2025). Network monitoring also uses semi supervised and active learning
(Pan, 2022). These approaches address different bottlenecks from testing whether a supplied
label grouping adds predictive information beyond ordinary blending.
Hierarchical and modular intrusion detection
Hierarchical classifiers exploit label trees (Silla & Freitas, 2011); intrusion models use family
to class staging (Han et al., 2026; Uddin et al., 2025). Extra parameters and routing can improve
predictions independently of an assignment. General classification therefore uses permuted
hierarchies as controls (del Moral et al., 2022; del Moral et al., 2023; Ojala & Garriga, 2009).
Our application combines those controls with rare class error accounting and grouped
assessment.
Taxonomy and ontology engineering
Taxonomy engineering evaluates structural quality (Kundisch et al., 2022; Nickerson et al.,
2013; Sampson et al., 2011). IoT security taxonomies organise overlapping threats differently
(Makhdoom et al., 2019; Xenofontos et al., 2022). Coherence or expert acceptance is not a
predictive test; we assess downstream rare detection utility.
Representation and deployment trends
Information systems applications include health data modeling (Cheng, 2022) and topology
based sinkhole detection (Mondal et al., 2022). Here numeric flow summaries permit controlled
assignment comparisons, but omit payloads, encrypted context, device identity, and online
adaptation. Application specific success alone does not validate taxonomy utility.
Validation, uncertainty, and evaluation practice
Evaluation must separate tuning from assessment and respect dependence among records
(López et al., 2014; Roberts et al., 2017). Permutation and mapping controls require their own
bias analysis (Golland et al., 2005; Ojala & Garriga, 2009). Effect sizes and intervals come
before test probabilities, and the holdout is the limiting unit of generalisation.
Operational evaluation adds calibration, alert budget, training cost, and edge constraints
(Alsubhi et al., 2008; Axelsson, 2000; Jamshidi et al., 2025; Roelofs et al., 2022; Tariq et al.,
2025). These are supporting evidence, not a production claim.
Benchmark context and research gap
We contribute a controlled rare IoT detection assessment, not hierarchy, permutation or
ensemble learning itself.

Wrong taxonomy controls
The cyclic map preserves family cardinalities while reassigning fine classes; support matching
preserves fitting support profiles. Controls receive the same models, roles, caps, grid, and
blending permissions.
Exceeding both controls supports local assignment value beyond tested hierarchy and support
alternatives, without excluding every flat ensemble or establishing semantic causation.
Pseudo taxonomy null controls
For each discovery holdout, ten pseudo mappings comprise four random size preserving
partitions, three KMeans partitions, and three diagonal Gaussian mixture partitions of fitting role
class centroids. Feature subsets have 8, 16, or 32 predictors. The adjusted Rand index measures
agreement with the real mapping. Similarity associations are descriptive within holdouts because
mappings share fitted data and assessment records.
The 60 nested maps are not independent datasets. A post review expansion adds 15 random
draws to seed 42's four existing maps. Each metric uses (1 + draws at least as large as the real
effect) / 20. Its minimum 0.05 is a finite conditional reference, not a population p value. The
draw family and model budget were fixed before observing added outcomes.
Data sources and roles
The Neto CICIoT2023 public subsample is distributed through Hugging Face from a Kaggle
mirror (Neto et al., 2023). Appendix A records provenance and vocabulary; packet to mirror
lineage was not independently reconstructed.
IoTID20 is the external replication corpus: 625,783 rows, 22 numeric predictors, and nine fine
labels (Ullah & Mahmoud, 2020). Its five family groups (Benign, Mirai, Scan, DoS, and
Spoofing) are obtained by grouping the fine labels on their documented attack family prefix; the
mapping adds no new semantic content and is supplied only to taxonomy models. Flat
comparators never see it.
The official archive contains 63 merged files with 45,019,234 valid rows, 34 labels, and 39
numeric predictors. It shares 37 predictors with the public subsample, adds two, and lacks nine
mirror only variables. Because vocabulary and processing differ, results from the two
distributions are never pooled (Table 2).
CICIoT2023 and IoTID20 remove nonfinite records before role assignment by exact predictor
group; session, campaign and device identifiers are unavailable. NF-ToN-IoT-v3 converts
nonfinite predictors to zero before hashing and modelling, so grouping matches classifier inputs.
N-BaIoT excludes device identity from predictors and holds out one device after uniform per file
sampling.

Repeated validation
Seeds 42 to 47 allocate predictor groups to 20 percent assessment, then divide development
70/15/15 into fitting, utility and calibration, and selection.
Fitting alone determines caps, weights, transformations, maps, and base models (Figure 1).
Utility and calibration fit gates and flat meta controls. Selection chooses settings and unknown
thresholds. Assessment outcomes determine none of them.
Fitting caps are 25,000 per attack class and 80,000 benign rows; uncapped fitting counts
freeze the rare set before caps.
IoTID20 uses ten 70/30 grouped holdouts under strict, modal, and modal 80 percent conflict
rules. Its rare set contains every class tied for minimum positive fitting support, not the
CICIoT2023 one percent set.

Models and comparators
Weighted multiclass XGBoost is the principal flat model (Chen & Guestrin, 2016);
LightGBM supplies family and local components (Ke et al., 2017). Appendix A lists frozen
constructors.
Comparators include weighted and unweighted trees, ordinary and temperature calibrated flat
blends, a converged Newton Cholesky stacker, hard and soft taxonomy models, and resampling
variants. Convergence diagnostics are retained. Tree budgets are matched only where stated,
without implying equal parameter counts.
Focal XGBoost uses 420 trees, focal exponent 2, and class weights with exponent 0.35.
Resampling variants target 5,000 eligible records per class before a LightGBM fit and are
tuned on the selection role (Ke et al., 2017).
Forests use 300 entropy trees. Histogram boosting selects iteration limits 20, 80, or 200 on
selection data; internal early stopping uses ten percent of fitting data. CatBoost uses selection
role stopping. The multilayer perceptron has 256 and 128 hidden units, a 50 iteration ceiling, and
internal fitting role stopping. Appendix A and the archived constructors specify the remaining
settings.

The estimand averages six paired split effects, not pooled row metrics. Conditional intervals
average six effects in each of 5,000 class stratified paired resamples of frozen predictions per
holdout (Efron, 1979). A second interval resamples contiguous 2,000 row blocks sorted by
original row identifier. Both condition on fitted models and observed assessment roles, not
unseen training sets or unavailable session clusters.
Approximate repeated holdout t intervals multiply the effect standard deviation by the square
root of 1/6 + 0.2/0.8, with five degrees of freedom (Nadeau & Bengio, 2003); unknown
campaign dependence remains unresolved. Overlapping holdouts are not independent signs: all
positive exact two sided binomial sign references are 0.03125. Holm adjustment covers eight
metric by contrast references. Exact McNemar correctness tests use four contrasts per holdout
and separate within holdout Holm adjustment (Ernst, 2004; McNemar, 1947), without pooling
minimum p values.
Macro average precision (AP) averages one versus rest average precision, using scikit learn
average_precision_score rather than trapezoidal PR area. Expected calibration error uses ten
equal width, right closed confidence bins, including zero in the first bin. Each occupied bin
contributes its row fraction multiplied by the absolute gap between top label accuracy and mean
confidence. Rare row ECE restricts the same calculation to true rare rows; it remains between
zero and one. Bin sensitivity and sparse rare support limit its interpretation (Roelofs et al., 2022;
Silva Filho et al., 2023). Multiclass Brier score sums squared class probability errors per row.
Repeated assessment counts are prediction instances, not unique records.
Implementation and reproducibility
The Windows workstation has eight physical CPU cores and 31.3 GB memory. Archived
versions are Python 3.12, XGBoost 3.3.0, LightGBM 4.6.0, CatBoost 1.2.10, scikit learn 1.9,
SciPy 1.18, and imbalanced learn 0.14.2. Added workers use two threads each; concurrent refit
durations are not deployment benchmarks.
Open set and label scarcity protocols
The added leave one family out analysis removes each of seven attack families from fitting,
utility and calibration, and known selection roles. Three grouped allocations give 21 cells.
Known class Macro F1 and rare recall assess classification; maximum softmax probability
assesses unknown flagging (Corsini & Yang, 2023; Geng et al., 2021; Scheirer et al., 2013).
Selection known rows determine thresholds for target false alarm rates of 1, 5, and 10 percent;
realised test false alarm rates are also reported. The held out family is never supplied as a fitted
output class.
The added scarcity experiment caps each fine class at 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, or 500 base fitting
rows across three grouped allocations. Both family exclusion and scarcity use 420 flat trees and
300 hierarchy trees, fixed before fitting. Utility and calibration labels remain available to every
arm, so caps are not total annotation budgets. Rare sets and assessment rows remain fixed.
Calibration and budget checks additionally reconstruct six frozen discovery holdouts with ten bin
ECE.
Sequential windows and drift protocol
The official files expose no timestamps. File order is therefore a disclosed capture order
proxy, not a chronological or concept drift evaluation.
Archive refits use files 1 through 45 for fitting, 46 through 54 for development, and windows
55 to 56, 57 to 58, 59 to 60, and 61 to 63 for assessment. Matched flat blends and stacking are
refitted on the archive's 39 predictors, never applied from a 46 predictor mirror model. Conflict
removal uses complete labels and is therefore a transductive integrity audit, not a prospective
cleaning procedure. Class and family Jensen Shannon divergence and population stability index
describe file order changes, not genuine temporal drift.
Language derived taxonomy prior
Qwen2.5 1.5B Instruct generated fixed technical descriptions at temperature zero; MPNet and
BGE sentence encoders converted them to 34 by 8 class to family cosine affinities at
temperatures 0.2, 0.5, and 1.0 (Song et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2025). These priors modify frozen
flat probabilities without retraining. This tests generic descriptions, not expert curation or threat
intelligence.
Encoder identifiers are all-mpnet-base-v2 and bge-small-en-v1.5; archived prompts and
outputs fix the description construction.
Independent source and genuine chronology
NF-ToN-IoT-v3 supplies 27,520,260 testbed flows with ten labels (Luay, 2025; Luay et al.,
2026). Its 53 nonlabel fields yield 48 predictors after excluding absolute start and end times, both
host addresses, and DNS transaction identifier. Nonfinite values become zero before hashing and
fitting; labels undergo no global consistency cleaning.
Seeds 42 to 44 allocate predictor hash groups, without labels, to fitting, calibration, selection,
and assessment with probabilities 0.56/0.12/0.12/0.20. Unequal groups change row fractions.
Fitting caps remain 25,000 per attack and 80,000 benign; deterministic label blind samples
bound calibration and selection at 60,000 each and assessment at 300,000 rows. All six role pairs
have zero shared hashes. Each allocation has one fitted seed.
A separate chronology splits genuine start times at their 60th, 75th, and 85th percentiles,
keeps ties together, and excludes flows crossing boundaries. Later roles purge earlier predictor
hashes without labels. Caps and samples are unchanged. Seeds 42 to 44 repeat fitting on this
single chronology. Primary Macro F1 includes all ten source labels, assigning zero to unlearned
classes; test supported scores are secondary. Empty fitting defined rare sets or absent test support
yield undefined rare recall, without fallback.
A researcher constructed partition from NF attack descriptions groups benign, availability,
input manipulation, interception and credentials, persistence and impact, and reconnaissance. It
is not an official dataset taxonomy or expert annotation. Each allocation rebuilds cyclic and
support controls from fitting data. Eleven arms comprise two flat learners, ordinary and
calibrated blends, stacking, and three hierarchies with their equal blends. Meta models use
calibration labels present during fitting, never future classes.
After inspecting chronological label support, a secondary frozen prediction audit uses one
minus maximum fitted class probability for unknown AUROC and average precision. No time
boundary, model, or threshold changes. Unknown prevalence is the constant score precision
reference; flagging differs from fine class recognition.
Standard informed control
CAPEC version 3.9 informs a researcher constructed 12 group mapping (MITRE, 2023).
Seven labels require broad or conditional matches; three web labels use an unresolved fallback.
This is not authoritative alignment or expert annotation. The same wrong partition is fitted in all
six holdouts, matching cardinalities and capped fitting support, not uncapped support or
parameter counts. All eight rare labels retain their named groups; only PingSweep changes its
companions. Credential Guessing, Input Injection, and Unresolved Web Compromise remain
intact. The control therefore cannot isolate semantic utility for every tail class.

Feature geometry and pseudo mappings
Against the real map's +0.0218 Macro F1 and +0.0827 rare recall, 24 random maps average
+0.0053 and +0.0023. Eighteen KMeans maps average +0.0133 and +0.0356; eighteen Gaussian
mixture maps +0.0075 and +0.0133. Feature partitions recover part, not all, of the mean margin.
The real map ranks first among the original 11 on rare recall in six holdouts and on Macro F1
in five. Its adjusted Rand index of one lies outside much of the pseudo map support. Weak
within holdout associations therefore cannot exclude similarity or identify a causal geometry
effect. The expanded reference assesses rank under one declared random mechanism. In the
frozen seed 42 expansion, 0/19 random maps match or exceed real Macro F1 and 0/19 match or
exceed rare recall. Their finite plus one tails are 0.05 and 0.05, respectively. The 0.05 resolution
and two endpoints preclude a broader significance claim. Figure 4 plots these draws, sorted
separately by Macro F1 on the left and rare recall on the right; horizontal lines mark the real map
in this holdout.

Scale and provenance: official archive
The official archive is a scale and integrity audit, not independent transfer. It contains
45,019,234 valid rows; 57.21 percent of rows in the final role are exact labelled repeats and
30.60 percent link to multiple labels. Cleaning needs complete labels to identify conflicts, so this
analysis bounds public subsample interpretation.
The archive has no timestamps, so sequential files describe a shifted capture order regime
rather than temporal drift. The blend remains above the flat model in four windows but does not
exceed the cyclic control, reinforcing the boundary on a general taxonomy claim.

DISCUSSION
What the protocol does and does not establish
The discovery gain survives the tested ensemble, capacity, support, calibration and
assignment controls, but six overlapping holdouts and positive conditional or approximate
intervals support only local conclusions. Feature, language and standard informed maps
constrain alternatives without identifying semantic causation. IoTID20 and archive reversals
preclude general architectural advice; N-BaIoT addresses device difficulty separately.
Maximum gate margins are approximately 0.0004 Macro F1 and 0.0039 rare recall at
incompatible grid points. The extra policy is therefore not recommended from these data.
Computational tradeoffs and deployment
The original workstation protocol uses seed 42 roles, one method per process, two warmups,
and seven timed predictions on 10,000 fixed rows (Figure 11). Training seconds and median
inference milliseconds are 414.8 and 1,181.0 for the taxonomy blend, 344.6 and 348.0 for
weighted flat, 674.4 and 1,955.2 for stacking, and 1,204.5 and 18,752.1 for tree budget matched
LightGBM. The compressed blend artifact occupies 78.33 MB. These are workstation costs, not
edge measurements.
Local triage value depends on budget: stacking retrieves more rare rows at 10,000 alerts.
Offline training costs do not establish gateway feasibility; a competitive flat alternative remains a
reasonable default.
Figure 11. Engineering cost
Limitations
The discovery mirror has incomplete packet lineage and differs from the official feature
vocabulary. Exact grouping blocks identical predictors but not unavailable sessions, campaigns,
or devices. Overlapping holdouts do not provide independent deployments, and approximate
repeated holdout intervals depend on a variance correction. The new independent source is still a
testbed, not operational traffic. N-BaIoT remains a device difficulty check rather than taxonomy
replication.
No physical edge device was available, so gateway latency, energy, memory, and throughput
remain unmeasured. Standard informed mappings are researcher constructions, not independent
expert annotations. Packet payloads, encrypted context, operational device metadata, multimodal
inputs, adversarial perturbations, and analyst workflow are untested. Chronology and grouped
random allocation answer different questions; an empty fitted rare set cannot be repaired by
choosing convenient time cutpoints. These limits restrict deployment and mechanism claims, not
the reporting of null or reversed effects.
DESIGN IMPLICATIONS FOR INFORMATION SYSTEMS PRACTICE
This assessment artifact follows design science (Akoka et al., 2023; Hevner et al., 2004;
March & Smith, 1995). It connects structural taxonomy criteria (Boudia & Bourahla, 2022;
Sangupamba Mwilu et al., 2024) with downstream categorisation quality (Valecha et al., 2025),
security artifact evaluation (Ebrahimi et al., 2025) and conditional design theory (Kane et al.,
2021). Table 14 operationalises local adoption: a competitive flat reference, useful effects
beyond equally privileged controls, acceptable cost and validation. Plausible names alone are
insufficient; external wrong map reversals can justify rejection. High conflict or weak alignment
prompts diagnosis, not automatic relabelling or proof that taxonomy cannot work.

CONCLUSION
For RQ1, six grouped discovery holdouts show local blending improvements in Macro F1
from 0.8739 to 0.8957 and rare recall from 0.5511 to 0.6338; approximate overlap adjusted
difference intervals are [0.0208, 0.0229] and [0.0769, 0.0885]. All six directions agree, but Holm
adjusted sign references remain inconclusive. Blending exceeds stacking on both primary metrics
but worsens all row calibration.
For RQ2, assignment specificity is local, without excluding mapping similarity or refuting
expert knowledge; gating adds little.
For RQ3, external representations, chronology and workstation costs constrain adoption;
production traffic and physical deployment remain unevaluated.

Protocol and source detail
Discovery development allocation uses seed offsets 10,000 and 20,000; caps and shuffle use
the split seed. Grouping precedes sampling. IoTID20 retains one representative per unique vector
under each conflict rule: strict removes conflicting vectors, modal assigns their modal label, and
modal 80 percent requires that agreement. Conflicts are representation properties, not established
annotation errors.
The Hugging Face release lacg030175/CIC-IoT-2023-neto-subsample, random configuration,
supplies 1,143,802 training and 285,951 test rows, retrieved in June 2026 and concatenated
before grouping. Its source card traces Kaggle akashdogra/ciciot23csv; packet lineage remains
unverified. IoTID20 uses third party release KathiS/IoTID20_Preprocessed_File. The official
archive uses Merged01.csv through Merged63.csv from cicresearch.ca. Byte sizes and
checksums remain in the review record.
The 37 shared predictors are Header_Length, Protocol Type, Rate, fin_flag_number,
syn_flag_number, rst_flag_number, psh_flag_number, ack_flag_number, ece_flag_number,
cwr_flag_number, ack_count, syn_count, fin_count, rst_count, HTTP, HTTPS, DNS, Telnet,
SMTP, SSH, IRC, TCP, UDP, DHCP, ARP, ICMP, IPv, LLC, Tot sum, Min, Max, AVG, Std,
Tot size, IAT, Number, Variance. Mirror only variables are flow_duration, Duration, Srate,
Drate, urg_count, Magnitue, Radius, Covariance, and Weight; archive only variables are
Time_To_Live and IGMP. Original source spellings are retained. The 37 predictor projection is
tested both within fixed original roles and with groups rebuilt after projection.
XGBoost uses 520 histogram trees, depth 9, rate 0.055, minimum child weight 2 and inverse
support exponent 0.35. Family and local LightGBM use 380 trees, 127 leaves, unrestricted depth,
minimum child support 25, rate 0.055 and weight exponents 0.25 and 0.50. Both constructors
specify row fraction 0.90, column fraction 0.95, L2 penalty 1.5 and L1 penalty 0.02; LightGBM
retains its default zero subsampling frequency. Flat LightGBM has 380 trees. Fitting support
determines weights and rarity before caps. Resampling and ranking use fitting only. The flat
stacker uses log probabilities, Newton Cholesky optimisation, C = 1 and at most 100 iterations,
with convergence checked. Temperatures range from 0.25 to 5.0. Identical information
permissions do not imply equal parameter counts.
Rare retrieval ranks rows by summed fitted rare class probability, dividing hits by true rare
rows rather than classes. Frozen budgets span 1,000 to 100,000 alerts plus fractional budgets;
outer benefits select none. Unknown flag thresholds and the extra calibration labels follow the
permissions described in Methods.
The tree budget flat comparator uses 2,660 LightGBM trees. Forests use square root feature
sampling and balanced weights; the balanced forest resamples all classes with replacement.
Histogram boosting uses 63 leaves, rate 0.08, L2 penalty 0.0001, and stopping patience 30.
CatBoost uses 520 iterations, depth 8, rate 0.08, square root balanced weights, and patience 40.
The standardised perceptron uses Adam, batches of 8,192, rate 0.001, penalty 0.0001, and
patience six. Borderline SMOTE and SMOTE ENN target 5,000 rows per eligible class with k =
min(5, smallest eligible support minus one); Borderline uses m = 10 and kind 1. ENN retains its
three neighbour default.

manifests remain in the review record.
